Why It Matters

The Day a Records Request Arrives.

Public records requests, audit notices, and compliance reviews don't give notice. The environment your records sit in determines what happens next.

Undocumented

Records scattered across folders nobody updated. Retention rules in someone's head. Staff scramble to compile a response. The clock is the enemy.

Structured

Records classified to a taxonomy your staff know. Retention rules documented and applied. Response compiled from a system designed for the question.

Scenarios

When the Question Gets Asked.

If someone asks who approved a payment, can we actually show it?

In this agency's case, yes — because approval became a required step rather than a habit. Before, physical purchase orders moved by hand and payments to local contractors and businesses slipped through the cracks. Now department heads receive a notification when an invoice is received, and a department head has to approve that invoice digitally before Accounts Payable generates a payment. The approvals, documents, and signatures are organized in one secure, cloud-based place. Watch the agency describe the process they run today.

We have to retain payroll records for years. Does going digital change that?

It does not change what you have to keep. It changes what keeping it costs you. A district told us its paper time cards had to be stored for extended periods for payroll calculation, and that hundreds of cards had turned into thousands, with individual cards regularly misplaced. We converted their paper time cards and records into digital files and built digital time cards inside the Microsoft apps they already owned. Watch the district describe the administrative burden that lifted.
